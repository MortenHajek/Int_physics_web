The sixteen chapters follow the course notes Introduction to Advanced Quantum Mechanics, in three parts: mathematical foundations (01–03), non-relativistic quantum mechanics (04–11), and relativistic quantum theory and fields (12–16). The treatment and the notation follow J. J. Sakurai and J. Napolitano, Modern Quantum Mechanics (2nd ed., Addison-Wesley, 2011), cited by section, and topics of Sakurai that the notes leave out are added where they belong: the Aharonov–Bohm effect, Bell’s inequality, lattice translations, the fine structure and Berry’s phase, the helium atom and the Dirac hydrogen atom. Sakurai stops short of the functional analysis of chapter 02 and of field theory, so chapters 02 and 14–16 follow the notes (marked “Course notes” with the chapter number), which draw on Reed and Simon, Hall, and Peskin and Schroeder. Sakurai’s notation: kets , eigenkets labelled by their eigenvalues, , the time-evolution operator , rotations , parity , time reversal and ensemble averages ; vectors carry arrows, as elsewhere on this site. Chapters 01–11 use SI units with written out (Sakurai uses Gaussian units; the formulas are converted), and from chapter 12 on , the metric and Heaviside–Lorentz units with , as in Sakurai’s chapter 8.
